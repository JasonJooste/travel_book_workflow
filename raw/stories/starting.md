This story starts with a kind stranger rescuing me from a mess of my own making. Just a small mess this time. A cloudy puddle to the oceans of mud to come, but illustrative nonetheless. About 10km out of the airport, 10km into a trip of 20,000, one 20th of one percent of the way, my right pedal fell off.  

Preparations had been a rush. Every day of work was precious. A few more coins in the purse for an expensive trip. In the end, in a fit of almost-suicidal optimism, I stopped work three days before my flight. In those three days, I had to sell all of my things, pack my bags, box up the rest for storage, say my (perhaps final) goodbyes and prepare the bike.

In the chaos, I ended up packing BoB (my beast of burden) about four hours before my evening flight. My toolbox was gone and I didn't have a spanner to remove the pedals. I rushed over to the neighbour's place and borrowed one. Then another problem. BoB has a big frame and didn't quite fit in the box. We hastily pushed the forks through the wall of the box and taped some extra cardboard around the protrusions. I had a rushed shower and glass of champagne and then was dropped at the airport by my long-suffering housemate, who luckily also worships at the altar of Discordia and mostly just found it funny.

But all this hurry in Brisbane left me in a tricky position on the other side. I was starting the trip in Munich, Germany, the gateway to Africa. I studied there for two years after driving there from Australia by motorbike, so it was the natural starting point for the next leg. I also insisted on riding BoB the *entire* way, starting with assembling her at the airport. Which required tools. Since my heroic neighbour had been selfish enough to take back his spanner, I didn't have a way to tighten the pedals. I'd already spent an hour ping-ponging between departments just trying to get rid of my cardboard bike box and wasn't particularly interested in playing sports equipment in another match. And so I screwed them in finger tight. 

Theoretically, they're oriented to tighten themselves while riding. Funny how the theories that mean less work are the ones that we embrace. It wasn't the last time that I would pay the price for this kind of motivated wishful thinking. 

And so, just out of the airport, the crank thread stripped completely and the pedal fell out. I rode the remaining 20km{{real value?}} with my right leg, with my left foot twisted inward awkwardly keeping the left crank spinning. It was a strange feeling to ride the bike paths of Munich, which I knew so well, in such a different context. Before, I'd used them on my commute to uni. Now they were just the minuscule start of an unfathomably long path, snaking its way half way across the world to South Africa. Before, I'd been riding in pure balance, now I risked looking like I'd invented leg wrestling. I was worried that this wasn't just a fun accident, a funny little twist at the start of the tale, but actually a sign of a clear lack of preparation. How could I possibly hope to travel 20,000km to Cape Town, when I couldn't travel 20km from the airport without a problem?

My friends were understandably horrified at the state of my equipment. My runners were already well-used and had holes in them. The bike was broken {{more??}}. I did a big shop at the sportsgoods store Decathlon (similar to Kathmandu in Australia) for all of the things that I'd forgotten {{list}}. It was time to leave and, with all my bags on the pavement, I fit the new crank on the bike. It was off by 45 degrees - an improvement on nothing at all, but probably not ideal. So my friends and I went on a walk.

This is where our kind stranger comes in. It turns out that BoB's bottom (bracket) has unusual dimensions. It's a square crank fitting {{check}}, but rotated at 45 degrees from the standard sizes. So we walked from bike shop to bike shop. None of them had our part. Until I reached a small boutique shop, where the kind owner offered had a second-hand crank that would fit. Despite my obvious desperation, he even offered it to me for free. BoB was back in business. 

After a few goodbyes, I was on the road. A friend of mine, Alex, is a keen bikepacker and joined me for the first day. He knows a lot about packing light and nutrition, so it was great to learn from him while we rode. He politely looked on as I filled my bags with kilos of apples, swam in my bike pants and pulled over every 10 mintues to check the map on my phone. His politeness cracked ever so slightly when I pulled out a stack of czech crowns and offered them to him. Why the fuck was I carrying the currency of a country I was not going to visit. And coins no less. Something in his toothbrush-cutting, tarp-camping, ultra-light brain broke. I left him behind the next day, dumbfounded. How was I possibly going to finish this thing?
